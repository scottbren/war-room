## T8: navmesh (risk prototype)
- Baker: `tools/src/navGeometry.ts` builds the triangle soup (flat floor slabs with holes for openings, wall and solid-cover blocks with a downward lid, closed windows, vertical links as off-mesh connections); `tools/src/bake-navmesh.ts` bakes one solo navmesh to `packages/content/generated/charon.navmesh.bin` (about 430 KB, about 2 s). A test checks the committed binary equals a fresh bake.
- **Deviation from the task text (agent 0.35 m, cell 0.2 m):** the baker uses cell 0.1 m and agent radius 0.3 m. With 0.2 m cells and 0.35 m radius every 1.0 and 1.2 m doorway in the map erodes shut (the wall faces already take a voxel on each side). Doors keep a clear width of door width minus 0.6 m.
- Cover 0.4 m high or lower (pool basins, kerbs) is stepped over; concealment (hedges, trees) is walkable; walls and cover are capped 0.2 m under the floor above so ground-floor walls cannot leak into the upper layer.

Floors that share a height (grounds/ground, hill/upper) are told apart by which floor has a room under the point.
- **createSim stays synchronous.** recast needs an async `init()`, so the caller (worker or test) awaits `createNav` and will pass the ready `Nav` into `createSim` as an option when T10 wires movement to it. Not wired yet; movement is still straight-line.
- Vertical links are plain off-mesh connections: `restrictedTo` (principal and family only) is not enforced by the mesh; the sim will enforce it.

## T9: line of sight (risk prototype)
- `sim/src/los.ts`: `createLos(map)` returns `{ sight(from, to, stance), setDoor, setShutter, cellValue }`; also `effectiveRange(rangeCm, concealed)` (concealment halves detection range, integer). Per-floor grids of 0.5 m cells; ray tracing is an integer Amanatides-Woo DDA on centimetre coordinates (no division in the loop).
- Cell values: 0 open, 1 low cover (0.41 to 1.59 m: hides a crouched target, not a standing one), 2 full block (walls, closed or locked doors, closed shutters, cover 1.6 m or taller). Cover 0.4 m or lower is ignored (same as the navmesh step-over rule). Concealment is a separate flag: never blocks, marks the target concealed if it stands in it or the ray passes through it.
- Walls rasterize along their centre line (so a thin wall costs one cell, not two); doors and shuttered windows are dynamic overlays on the wall gap. A ray that passes exactly through a cell corner needs both side cells open (no squeezing between two blocked cells). A segment that ends exactly on a corner is handled (found by a test).
- Vertical sight: only through an upper-floor opening whose `below` is the other floor, and only to a point under the opening. The observer is projected to the nearest point of the void; leg 1 (on the upper floor, low cover ignored: a balustrade is looked over) must be clear, leg 2 is traced on the lower floor with normal rules. Works both ways.
- Diagonal walls are refused (the map has none; they would otherwise fill their whole bounding box).
- Known limits: sight does not yet use elevation (a roof observer cannot look down through anything), and positions exactly on a wall line are ambiguous by design (units are never placed inside walls).
- Not wired into the pipeline yet (perception system comes later); door and shutter changes will call `setDoor` and `setShutter` from the sim systems.

## T10: client and worker bridge
- **Nav is passed into `createSim` as an optional `nav` (not saved state).** With a nav, `move` computes a route (`findPath`) and the unit follows the waypoints; an unreachable target is rejected with a `commandRejected` event ("no route to target"). Without one, movement stays the old straight line (so existing tests are unchanged). `arrived` fires once, when the whole route ends.
- Floor changes along a route (stairs, lift, tunnel) happen as a pop on arrival at the link end: the unit walks to the link on the source floor, then appears on the target floor. Smooth stair animation is later polish.
- Worker protocol (`client/src/protocol.ts`): page to worker `init {seed}` and `moveTo {entity, candidates[]}`; worker to page `ready {snapshot}`, `tick {snapshot, events}`, `error {message}`. A click is sent as one candidate per visible floor (nearest along the camera ray first); the worker uses the first one the navmesh calls walkable, so clicking where a roof is hidden or absent falls through to the floor below.
- The worker steps at 10 Hz with `setInterval`; commands are enqueued with the last completed tick so they apply on the next step. The page keeps the last two snapshots and interpolates over the measured tick interval (clamped to 50-400 ms), jumping when a unit is new or changes floor.
- Engine: WebGPU when `WebGPUEngine.IsSupportedAsync` and init succeeds, otherwise WebGL2; `?gl=webgl2` forces the fallback. Overlay shows backend, fps, tick.
- Debug scenario: `scenario.ts` adds one operator (`op.alpha.1`) on the helipad (48, 68). Real spawns come with mission data.
- Verified in Chromium through Playwright: the operator walks helipad to study on both WebGPU and WebGL2, reaching (54, 37) exactly.

## T11: camera effects and performance
- **Effects are full-screen `PostProcess`es with the shader written twice** (GLSL in `ShaderStore.ShadersStore`, WGSL in `ShadersStoreWGSL`, same name) and `shaderLanguage` picked from the backend. Night vision, grain, fisheye and thermal all compile and render on WebGPU and WebGL2 (`client/src/fx/effects.ts`).
- **Thermal: choose approach (b), swapping materials in place, as the default; keep (a) for picture-in-picture.** Measured on the GTX 1060: (b) costs about +0.1 ms, (a) (a second full render of the scene with replacement materials) about +0.7 to +0.9 ms. (b) is cheaper because no extra pass is drawn; (a) leaves the main view untouched, so it is the one to use if a thermal image must be shown while the normal view stays visible (a helmet cam in thermal). The heat value per mesh comes from its material key (`fx/heat.ts`: people 1.0, concealment 0.3, water 0.05-0.1, slab 0.3, etc.), snapped to five buckets so five shared materials cover every mesh.
- **Helmet cams: 320x180 render targets, rotate the refresh** (one camera per frame). Every-frame costs +1.7 ms (WebGL2) and +3.1 ms (WebGPU); rotating costs +1.0 ms on both. Shown as a 2x2 overlay of planes parented to the main camera in rendering group 1.
- Measurement method and numbers: `docs/perf-notes.md`.

## 2026-11-04: Perception and the first knowledge layer (no change to Snapshot, Command or SimEvent)
- **Perception system** (`sim/src/systems/perception.ts`, now in its pipeline slot): operators watch everyone who is not theirs; defenders watch operators. Each observer checks every 3 ticks (staggered by rank), using the line-of-sight grid, a 20 m range (halved if the target is concealed) and integer squared distances. A sighting emits the existing `contact` event, and repeats it every check while the target stays in view; losing sight emits nothing. No facing or field of view yet. Sight memory is in the sim state as `sight`, written only while someone is in view, so quiet runs hash as before. The one regression-pinned hash in `sim.test.ts` was re-pinned because its scenario has an operator and a defender in view of each other.
- **Line of sight fixes found while testing** (`los.ts`): (1) windows now let sight through (derived walls are solid across windows, so the open window cells are cleared and a closed shutter blocks them again); (2) floors at the same height share one grid, so someone on the outdoor `grounds` floor can see into the house's `ground` floor through a window (and the hill and the upper floor likewise). Different heights still see each other only through voids. The CHARON replay (T12) is unchanged.
- **Knowledge layer** (`sim/src/knowledge.ts`, `createKnowledge(map, side)`): derived data, not sim state. Fed each tick's snapshot and events, it holds one side's picture: its own units, anyone it has had contact with (live for 6 ticks after the last contact, then a last-known position), and the door plan (every door except hidden ones nobody has revealed; `revealDoor` is the hook for searches and intel). It never reports a unit it has not seen. The snapshot is used only to read a contacted unit's position at the moment of contact.
- **Client:** the viewer now draws the operators' knowledge, not truth: own units, units in view, and faded last-known positions. Key T switches to the raw truth for debugging. Known gap: a live unit is drawn from interpolated truth for up to about 0.6 s after it leaves view; the hidden-door panel and the whole greybox are still drawn from the full map (the dev view), so the blueprint filter has no visual effect yet.

## 2026-11-05: Hearing, and a new `noise` event (contract change, approved by Scott)
Scott approved adding a `noise` event to the public `SimEvent` type, and the order: hearing, then the defender state machine, then facing and field of view, then evidence and comms.
- **Event:** `{ type: "noise"; observer: EntityId; at: Pos; kind: "steps" | "door" }`. `at` is an approximate spot, wrong by up to a quarter of the distance (drawn from the seeded PRNG). The source is never named. Also added to `docs/contracts-draft.md`.
- **Rules** (`sim/src/systems/hearing.ts`, run inside the perception slot, no saved state): footsteps come from units that moved this tick and carry speed x 40 cm (operator 8 m, defender 6 m); a listener checks for footsteps every 5 ticks (staggered by rank). A door changing state is heard at once out to 12 m. With no line of sight to the sound it carries half as far; through a floor, a third. Same sides as sight: operators hear non-operators, defenders hear operators; nobody hears themselves.
- **Knowledge:** `Knowledge.noises()` returns the last 32 sounds heard by that side's own units (`KnownNoise`: where, kind, tick). Additive API only.
